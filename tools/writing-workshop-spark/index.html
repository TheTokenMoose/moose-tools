Fantasy narrative · Who · What · Where

In workshop narrative units, writers invent a character (who), a problem or adventure (what), and a place (where). Spin or pick a card from each column to seed a story, then draft with a beginning, middle, and end.

        Writer’s craft cues

        • Show the character wanting something at the start.
        • Let the problem get a little worse before it gets better.
        • Use the setting to create mood (foggy, glowing, noisy, quiet).
        • End with a small change in the character or the place.

        How-to · Procedural writing

        Information / procedural units teach writers to explain a process clearly for a reader who has never done it. Choose a topic, then build ordered steps with materials, warnings, and a “done when…” ending.

        Topic

        Topic bank

          Step frames (sequence words)

            Writer’s craft cues

            • Start with who this is for and what they will be able to do.
            • List materials before the steps.
            • Use sequence words: first, next, then, after that, finally.
            • Add a tip or warning where a reader might get stuck.
            • End with how the reader will know they finished successfully.

            Opinion / persuasive writing

            Opinion units ask writers to take a clear position for a real audience, support it with reasons, and back reasons with examples. Use the boards below to choose a claim, audience, reasons, and evidence starters.

                        Writer’s craft cues

                        • State the opinion in one clear sentence near the start.
                        • Give at least two or three distinct reasons.
                        • For each reason, add an example, fact, or small story.
                        • Name the audience and choose words that respect them.
                        • Close by restating the opinion and a call to action or hope.
