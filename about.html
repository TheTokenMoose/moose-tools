About The Token Moose

The Token Moose is a small collection of games, experiments, and teacher tools made by a teacher who likes building things.

I enjoy finding new ways to make learning more fun, interactive, and engaging, while also giving myself an excuse to keep learning. These projects are where those interests overlap — a mixture of educational games, little classroom tools, experiments with code, AI-assisted development, and whatever interesting idea I happen to be playing with next.

Some of these projects were made to solve a problem I encountered in my own classroom. Others started as a "could I make this?" idea. Some are simply experiments that turned out to be fun.

I'm collecting them here so they're easy to play, use, share, and explore.

Everything here is made with the hope that someone else might find it useful, entertaining, or inspiring. The projects are open source, so you're welcome to look at how they work, learn from them, modify them, or take them in an entirely different direction.

Have fun. Break things. Make things. Keep learning.

About Me

Hi, I'm David Templeton, although around the internet you'll usually find me as The Token Moose.

I'm a teacher, lifelong learner, and technology enthusiast. I've spent years working in education, particularly with young learners and English language learners, and I'm always interested in finding better ways to make learning active, memorable, and enjoyable.

I'm also the sort of person who looks at a repetitive classroom task and thinks, "I wonder if I could just make a little program to do this?"

That habit has led me down some interesting rabbit holes.

I enjoy experimenting with Python, web development, AI tools, game design, 3D printing, and whatever new technology happens to catch my attention. I'm not a professional game developer or software engineer — I'm a teacher who enjoys learning by building things.

That's really what The Token Moose is about.

These projects give me somewhere to experiment, practice coding, explore AI-assisted development, solve problems I encounter in teaching, and occasionally make something just because it sounds fun.

Some projects will be polished. Some will be weird. Some will probably be both.

They're all part of the learning process.

— David Templeton / The Token Moose
