About Moose Tools

A workshop on the internet — games, tools, and experiments built to make things.

The workshop

Moose Tools is my little workshop on the internet — a growing collection of games, experiments, creative projects, and useful tools built around one simple idea: make things.

I'm a teacher, but I'm also a maker. I like taking an idea from “I wonder if I could…” to something that actually exists.

Sometimes that means building a classroom game. Sometimes it's a tool to make a teacher's job easier. Sometimes it's a website, a Python program, a 3D-printed object, a digital experiment, or an idea that has absolutely no practical purpose other than being fun to make.

A lot of the projects here grow out of real problems and opportunities I've encountered in education. If something could make a lesson more engaging, give students another way to practise something, save a teacher ten minutes, or simply make a classroom a little more interesting, I'm probably going to try building it.

Moose Tools is also a place for experimentation. I use code, web technologies, AI-assisted development, game design, 3D modelling, 3D printing, and whatever else I happen to be learning to turn ideas into things I can actually use.

Some projects are polished. Some are experiments. Some are works in progress.

That's intentional.

This isn't a software company or a commercial product catalogue. It's a workshop — a place to build, learn, test ideas, break things, fix them, and share the results.

The projects are open source because I believe that making things is even more useful when other people can take them apart, learn from them, improve them, or build something completely different from them.

Make something. Try something. Break something. Learn something. Then make something better.

About me

I'm David Templeton — a teacher, maker, tinkerer, and lifelong learner. Online, you'll usually find me as The Token Moose.

Teaching is a huge part of who I am, but making things has always been a big part of how I learn.

I like figuring things out by actually building them. I'll learn some Python because I have an idea for a program. I'll learn web development because I want to put an idea online. I'll design a 3D model because I can't find quite the right object. I'll experiment with AI because I want to see what it can help me create.

Then I'll inevitably end up going down a completely unrelated rabbit hole because I wondered “Could I make that too?”

That's how I ended up exploring things like game development, web design, programming, AI-assisted development, 3D modelling, 3D printing, digital tools, educational technology, and creative design.

What makes it particularly interesting to me is being able to bring those skills back into education.

Being a teacher means I'm constantly seeing opportunities to improve the experience of learning. A game can turn practice into play. A simple tool can remove a frustrating repetitive task. A visual resource can help an idea click. A little bit of technology can sometimes give a student another way to understand something.

And being a learner means I'm still figuring things out myself.

I don't consider myself a professional software engineer, game developer, industrial designer, or any of the other hats I occasionally wear while making these projects. I'm a teacher who enjoys learning new skills by using them to make things.

Moose Tools is where all of those interests collide.

It's my workshop, playground, laboratory, and occasionally a place where I can finally finish the project that started with “This should only take an afternoon.”

If something here helps a teacher, gives a student a more enjoyable way to learn, inspires someone to make something themselves, or simply makes someone smile, then it's done its job.

— David Templeton / The Token Moose
